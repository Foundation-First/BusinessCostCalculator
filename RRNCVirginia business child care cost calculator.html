    Where each rate comes from

    These citations come from the Blanco Center brief. We did not review the original studies.

    • 14 days missed per parent a year (112 hours at 8 hours a day): LPIC’s Losing Ground study (2017). The 8-hour day is our reading of the 112 hours.
    • 2,087 working hours a year: U.S. Office of Personnel Management’s hourly-rate divisor.
    • 2.8% of parents quit over child care: Montes and Halterman (2011), Academic Pediatrics.
    • Replacement cost of 21.4% of salary: Boushey and Glynn (2012).
    • Added cost of absences, 20.9%: Society for Human Resource Management (2014).

    How this works

    How the estimate is calculated

    The estimate adds up three costs. Each one uses your average wage, which is total payroll divided by employees.

    1. Missed work. Each parent misses 112 hours a year (14 days of 8 hours). Cost is parents × 112 ÷ 2,087 working hours × average wage.
    2. Added costs of absences. 20.9% on top of missed wages, for coworker catch-up, HR time, and overtime.
    3. Replacing people who leave. 2.8% of parents quit over child care, and each departure costs 21.4% of a salary to replace.

    Total = parents × (112 ÷ 2,087) × average wage × 1.209, plus parents × 2.8% × average wage × 21.4%. In practice that is about 7.1% of the yearly wages of your parent employees (our arithmetic from the Louisiana equations, not a published figure).

    The rates come from the Louisiana Policy Institute for Children’s research: its 2017 Losing Ground study for the 14 days, a national study for the 2.8%, a workforce study for the 21.4%, and a human resources survey for the 20.9%. Virginia-specific rates were not available when this was built.

    How this differs from the Louisiana calculator
    • The equations and rates are the same.
    • Expected departures are not rounded up. The Louisiana calculator always rounds up, so a workplace with 50 parents is charged for 2 departures instead of 1.4. That overstates costs for small employers. Use the checkbox under “Change the assumptions” to match it.
    • Hourly wages are not rounded to the cent partway through, which keeps small amounts exact.
    • If you do not know your number of parents or your payroll, this tool fills in an estimate for your area instead of requiring both.
    What this does not tell you
    • It is an estimate, not a forecast. Your own absence and turnover records will beat these averages.
    • Parents are assumed to earn your average wage.
    • It counts costs to your workplace only, not ripple effects on the wider economy.
    • Parent shares come from survey samples. Their 90% margins of error run from about 0.2 points for Virginia to 2.4 points for Rural East RRNC, and the page shows each one. Average pay in places with few jobs can swing with a single employer.
